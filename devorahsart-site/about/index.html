Who is Devorah?

Devorah is a young Israeli artist who works in bold acrylics, turning everyday moments into expressive portraits influenced by anime and classical figure painting.

She is mostly self-taught and works at home in Israel, drawing commute scenes, family portraits, wild-haired characters, and tender objects like teddy bears. Her brushwork is loose and honest rather than polished and distant. Buyers who want art from a real young artist rather than anonymous stock imagery come to Devorah's Art for exactly that reason.

Which piece shows how Devorah sees herself?

Open Letter is the closest thing Devorah has made to a self-portrait: a surreal circular portrait in her own expressive style, with a straw hat, flowers, and a sealed letter where thoughts would be.

It is not a literal likeness, but it captures how she sees herself as an artist: half calm, half overflowing with color and ideas, always holding something she has not quite said yet. The sketchy lines and playful surrealism are her signature. If you want to understand her visual voice before browsing the full shop, start with Open Letter.

Why does Devorah sell digital downloads instead of physical prints?

Digital downloads keep traditional scans at $5 and digital compositions at $3, and let buyers anywhere in the world print at their preferred size without shipping costs or delays.

Physical print-on-demand adds production markup and weeks of waiting. By scanning paintings in high resolution and delivering files by email, Devorah removes those barriers. You choose the paper, the frame, and the size, from a desk print to a statement wall piece.

Where does Devorah create her art?

Most of Devorah's art is made at home: drawings and paintings at her desk or easel, digital work on her laptop. About 90% of her skill is self-taught. She only recently started going to an art studio twice a month.

The shop is a curated selection, not everything she makes. Older sketchbook pieces sit alongside newer paintings, and Devorah deliberately keeps some work just for herself to avoid fatigue from sharing every sketch. When something appears here, it is because she genuinely wants to put it out into the world. Studio photos on this page are from those twice-monthly visits, including finished work like The Woman on the Bus.
